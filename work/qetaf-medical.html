Qetaf Medical Replatforming an Arabic-first medical store

Role
UX/UI Designer
Platform
Custom web app on WooCommerce, replacing Salla
Tools
FigJam · Figma · Zeplin

Context

Qetaf Medical sells medical equipment and supplies to a broad Arabic-speaking base — caregivers of elderly parents, diabetic patients, people with mobility or orthopedic needs, parents of children with disabilities, and clinics and hospitals buying at scale. Many shoppers are buying for someone else, not themselves, and often aren't sure exactly what product fits the need. The store ran on Salla, a template-based platform that limited how far the experience could be shaped around how these customers actually shop.

Problem

Salla's fixed templates forced a generic structure onto a business that needed something more deliberate: a catalogue organized around medical situations rather than a flat category tree, installment and cash-on-delivery options visible early rather than buried in checkout, and an RTL experience authored for Arabic reading rather than mirrored from an LTR template.

My Role

UX/UI designer, working within a broader delivery structure that included a PM and an agency alongside the client and dev team. I owned requirements gathering, segmentation and persona research, competitive analysis, information architecture, wireframes, and high-fidelity RTL UI, handed off to development via Zeplin.

Key Decisions

  • Rebuilt the IA around medical situations — mobility, diabetic care, orthopedic recovery, home care — instead of a generic supplier category tree, since many shoppers arrive knowing their situation, not the product name.
  • Designed for the reality that most buyers are shopping for someone else — a parent, a patient, an aging relative — so clear specs and trust signals mattered more than lifestyle imagery.
  • Surfaced installment and cash-on-delivery options on the product page, not only at checkout, since affordability — not product quality — was the real hesitation for considered healthcare purchases.
  • Designed right-to-left as the native reading direction from the ground up, rather than mirroring an LTR template, down to spacing, iconography, and form field order.
  • Made WhatsApp a core touchpoint — OTP login, order updates, abandoned-cart recovery — since it's the channel most of these customers already trust.
  • Simplified visual hierarchy and enlarged type and tap targets for an audience that skews less digitally confident.

Outcome

The site hasn't launched yet. I built an interactive prototype and tested it with users from the target segments; the sessions showed clear improvement over the old Salla experience — faster navigation, quicker product discovery, and less confusion around fit — validating the new structure before development.

Reflection

Replatforming off a template isn't just a redesign — it's reconciling priorities that don't naturally agree: the client's business goals, real usability for a wide and varied audience, SEO requirements, and what the dev team's stack could realistically support. Most of the work was making sure no single priority won at the expense of the others.
