B2B · Travel Tech

VBooking Turbo Suite Unifying booking, CRM, and AI for travel agents

Role
Lead UX/UI Designer, solo end-to-end
Platform
Web app — Booking Engine, CRM, Itinerary Builder, Ask Genie
Tools
Figma · FigJam

Context

VBooking set out to unify a fragmented industry — wholesalers, DMCs, OTAs, and holiday rental managers — under one booking platform, Turbo Suite. Agents were used to juggling separate tools for search, quoting, CRM, and finance across every supplier and channel they worked with.

Problem

Agents were losing time and revenue to problems baked into how the industry worked: supplier management and commission structures were manually tangled, inventory wasn't updated in real time so agents quoted stale prices, and once a lead came in, there was no single place to track it from first contact to payment. Support tools were too weak to hold onto the customer relationship, let alone upsell.

My Role

Lead UX/UI designer, solely responsible for the end-to-end design of Turbo Suite — from the CRM pipeline to the itinerary builder to Ask Genie — working as the only designer across a fast-moving, multi-team build.

Key Decisions

  • Built CRM pipelines that pull every incoming lead into one Kanban view (New → Sent → Follow ups → Viewed → Paid), so an agent never checks two systems to know where a deal stands.
  • Put real-time inventory and financial exposure — unpaid orders, contract limits, cancellations — on the home dashboard itself, not buried in a report, since stale numbers were the exact complaint agents had about the old way of working.
  • Designed the itinerary builder day-by-day — flights, hotels, restaurants, transport per day — with a "Generate with AI" shortcut, so an agent could go from a client's ask to a priced multi-day package in minutes instead of assembling it supplier by supplier.
  • Made Ask Genie a first-class tab in the workspace, not a side panel, so agents could describe what a customer needed in plain language and get offers back immediately.
  • Consolidated search, CRM, finance, and tools under one sidebar instead of separate logins per function, removing the tool-switching agents were already stuck with.

Outcome

Turbo Suite shipped as a live beta and is currently in use by agents, though it hasn't opened to the general market yet. Working with real agents at this stage surfaced exactly where the old tool-hopping workflow still leaked into the new one — feedback now shaping the run-up to a full launch.

Reflection

The biggest lesson was how differently "good design" reads on the B2B side: agents don't want delight, they want speed and control over their own numbers, so every decision got tested against "does this save a step" rather than "does this look good." Heavy competitor analysis across travel-tech platforms is also what pushed decisions like the unified CRM pipeline, instead of just copying what every other booking tool already did.
